I practiced a bunch of pieces to let people study with comfortable background music. This was mainly focused on minecraft music (all songs from the main game soundtrack but not music disks) and similar types of songs - for instance Gymnopedie. I also threw in a small smattering of songs that I knew how to play just to spice it up. I poured a lot of time into preparing for the performance, even though I was doing it as a favor to the hmc Muchachos (a community group trying to get people to socalize). The only people who showed up were my two bests friends - the only two students I told about it - my dad and brother, the muchachos (who organize the event), and two people who were interested. This made me quite sad, realizing the state of the world that we are in. People feel too busy to sit and enjoy music, even though all advertising for it was aimed at the study-orientedness of the music. The songs that I played were all focused on the sense of nostalgia - this is music that we first heard when we were around the ages of 10-13, and which was very foundational to many of our lives beyond then. Nostalgia for our childhoods, the peaceful moments when nothing really mattered and we got to waste time doing busy labor in a video game with no end goal. And yet, there were very few people who still felt the same freedom that we had as children - to appreciate not doing anything "work" related or "productive" for an hour, to listen to music. 

It is easy to disparage a generation for loosing the good old things, and there was likely a gap in advertising that the event was happening in the first place. I think I also misunderstood the goal of music - I was trying too much to meet people where I percieved they were at. People want to maximize their use out of free time, people want to feel happy instead of sad and nostalgic. Events with rock or music tend to have a much greater showing. But even still, I find this need for stimulation to engage with music a pernicious condition of my peers.
