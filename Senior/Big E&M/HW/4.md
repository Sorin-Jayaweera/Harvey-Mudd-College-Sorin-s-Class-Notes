 Let $\varphi=\phi$, the electric potential. If we represent $\phi$ (electric potential) as $\phi=G(r,r') +F(r,r')$, then let $\psi= G(r,r')$.

In a vacuum, there is no charge density, so $\nabla^{2}\phi=0$ unless we have a free particle at the origin.
$$
\begin{align}
\nabla^{2}G = 4\pi\delta^{3}(x-x')
\end{align}
$$

$G = \frac{1}{\left| \vec{r}-\vec{r}' \right|}$, where $\vec{r}'=\vec{0}$.

We have the boundary condition that at some radius $R$ of a sphere we are enforcing some $\phi_{0}(\theta,\phi)$.

We want to show that
$$
\begin{align}
\frac{1}{4\pi R^{2}} \oint \phi(R,\theta,\phi) \cdot da = \phi(r=0)
\end{align}
$$


We have green's function

$$
\begin{align}
\int\varphi \nabla^{2}\psi - \psi \nabla^{2}\varphi  & = \oint \varphi \vec{\nabla}\psi - \psi \vec{\nabla}\varphi \\
\int \phi (4\pi\delta^{3}(\vec{r}))  - \frac{1}{\left| \vec{r} \right| } 0 dv & = \oint \varphi \vec{\nabla}\psi - \psi \vec{\nabla}\varphi \cdot da \\
4\pi\phi(\vec{r}) &  = \oint \phi \frac{ d }{d r }  \left( \frac{1}{\left| r \right| } \right) - 0 \\ 
4\pi\phi(\vec{r}) &  = \oint \frac{\phi}{r^{2}} \\ 
\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi R^{2}} \oint \phi \cdot da
\end{align}
$$

QED
