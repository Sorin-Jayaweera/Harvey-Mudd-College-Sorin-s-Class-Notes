\Phi_{E}=  \oint \oint _{s} E\cdot dA =  \frac{Q_{enc}}{\epsilon_{0}}  
\end{align}
$$
The electric field is perpendicular to the top / bottom ends of the cube everywhere, and parallel to the sides. If $s$ is the side length of the square face of the rectangle, the flux is just
$$
\begin{align} 
Q_{enc}   & = \sigma s^{2}\\
E \cdot 2 s^{2}  & = \frac{Q_{enc}}{\epsilon_{0}}  \\
E  & = \frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_{0}}
\end{align}
$$
Each of the two plates has this field, so we have
$$
\begin{align}
E_{+} = \frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_{0}} \\
E_{-} = -\frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_{0}}  
\end{align}
$$

The positive field is pulling negative charges towards itself, and the negative field is pushing away from itself. The fields must therefore have a higher amplitude between them (it can't be zero), so we have
$$
\begin{align}
E = \frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_{0}}
\end{align}
$$

$$

\end{align}

$$
\begin{align}
C=\frac{Q}{V} = \frac{\epsilon_{0}Q}{\sigma d}
