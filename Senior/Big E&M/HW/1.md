### Parity
EQ 4.21
$$
\begin{align}
j^{\mu'}= P^{\mu'}_{\mu} j^{\mu}
\end{align}
$$

Eq 4.22
$$
\begin{align}
\partial^{\mu'}= P^{\mu'}_{\mu} \partial^{\mu} 
\end{align}
$$
$$
\begin{align}
F^{\mu'\nu'}=  P^{\mu'}_{\mu} P^{\nu'}_{\nu} F^{\mu \nu}
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
\partial_{\mu} F^{\mu \nu}= \frac{4\pi}{c}j^{\nu} 
\end{align}
$$
We can transform this under parity

$$
\begin{align}
P^{\mu'}_{\mu} \partial_{\mu'} F^{\mu \nu}= \frac{4\pi}{c}P^{\nu}_{\nu'} j^{\nu'}
\end{align}
$$
We can multiply both sides with the inverse 

$$
\begin{align}
P^{\rho'}_{\nu}P^{\mu'}_{\mu}   \partial_{\mu'} F^{\mu\nu}= P^{\rho'}_{\nu} \frac{4\pi}{c}P^{\nu}_{\nu'} j^{\nu'} \\
= \delta^{\rho'}_{\nu'} \frac{4\pi}{c} J^{\nu'} \\
\partial_{\mu'} (P^{\rho'}_{\nu} P^{\mu'}_{\mu} F^{\mu \nu})= \frac{4\pi}{c} j^{\rho'}
\end{align}
$$
This looks the same as before on the right side, so the internal part
$$
\begin{align}
P^{\nu'}_{\nu} P^{\mu'}_{\mu} F^{\mu \nu}= F^{\mu'\nu'}
\end{align}
$$

Let's just directly apply parity to the components of $F$. The parity operator swaps the sign of the last three components in a four vector. Therefore, we swap the three columns going right and the three rows going down. The innermost items are hit twice, whereas the edges are only hit once. Therefore,
$$
\begin{align}
F^{\mu'\nu'} & = P^{\mu'}_{\mu} P^{\nu'}_{\nu} \begin{pmatrix}
0 & -E^{1} & -E^{2} & -E^{3} \\
E_{1} & 0 & B^{3} & -B^{2} \\
E^{2} & -B^{3} & 0 & B^{1}  \\
E^{3} & B^{2} & -B^{1} & 0
\end{pmatrix} \\
 & = \begin{pmatrix}
0 & E^{1} & E^{2} & E^{3} \\
-E_{1} & 0 & B^{3} & -B^{2} \\
-E^{2} & -B^{3} & 0 & B^{1}  \\
-E^{3} & B^{2} & -B^{1} & 0
\end{pmatrix}
\end{align}
$$

We can see that this has only swapped the components of the electric field, whereas the magnetic field has stayed the same. Therefore, under parity:
$$
\begin{align}
E'=-E, B'=B
\end{align}
$$

### Time reversal

Under time reversal transformation
$$
\begin{align}
j^{\mu'}= -T^{\mu'}_{\mu} j^{\mu} \\
\partial^{\mu'}= + T^{\mu'}_{\mu} \partial^{\mu}
\end{align}
$$

Lets write out Maxwell's equations
$$
\begin{align}
\partial_{\mu} F^{\mu \nu} = \frac{4\pi}{c}j^{\nu} \\
\partial_{\mu'} F^{\mu'\nu'}= \frac{4\pi}{c} j^{\nu'}
\end{align}
$$
The derivative only gets a $T$, but the $J$ gets a $-$. Therefore, we have an additional minus sign at the beginning, but it looks the same as the parity bit. We can jump to:
$$
\begin{align}
F^{\mu'\nu'} & = -T^{\mu'}_{\mu} T^{\nu'}_{\nu} \begin{pmatrix}
0 & -E^{1} & -E^{2} & -E^{3} \\
E_{1} & 0 & B^{3} & -B^{2} \\
E^{2} & -B^{3} & 0 & B^{1}  \\
E^{3} & B^{2} & -B^{1} & 0
\end{pmatrix} \\
 & = -\begin{pmatrix}
0 & E^{1} & E^{2} & E^{3} \\
-E_{1} & 0 & B^{3} & -B^{2} \\
-E^{2} & -B^{3} & 0 & B^{1}  \\
-E^{3} & B^{2} & -B^{1} & 0
\end{pmatrix} \\
 & =\begin{pmatrix}
0 & -E^{1} & -E^{2} & -E^{3} \\
E_{1} & 0 & -B^{3} & B^{2} \\
E^{2} & B^{3} & 0 & -B^{1}  \\
E^{3} & -B^{2} & B^{1} & 0
\end{pmatrix} \\
\end{align}
$$
We have swapped the $E$ components twice, and the $B$ components only once.

Therefore, under parity
$$
\begin{align}
\vec{E}'=\vec{E}, \vec{B}'=-\vec{B} 
\end{align}
$$
